## Questions

1. Where did you put business logic — controller or service — and why?
2. How do you test a route manually with curl or REST Client?

---

## Answers

1. Service is the centralise area for business logic.

Controllers handle HTTP layer (request routing, validation and response).

Keep all logic in service is good for testing and reuseable for different controllers.

2. Basic GET: `curl -v http://localhost:8080/api/users`

POST:

```
curl -X POST http://localhost:8080/api/users \
-H "Content-Type: application/json" \
-d '{"name": "Alice", "role": "admin"}'
```

### Reviewer comments

- **Q1:** Correct Nest layering — **controller** = transport (route, status, DTO in/out); **service** = rules and orchestration. Testing and reuse are the right “why.” **Polish:** validation often lives at the boundary (`ValidationPipe` + DTO on the controller) while **decisions** (not found, merge PATCH, pagination) stay in the service; later, persistence moves to a repository, not back into the controller.
- **Q2:** Solid manual checks — `-v` for headers/status, POST with `Content-Type` and JSON body. **Next:** add `PATCH`/`DELETE` one-liners; use `-i` or `-w "%{http_code}"` when you care about 201 vs 400; mirror these in `docs/requests.http` for REST Client. Docs: [NestJS controllers](https://docs.nestjs.com/controllers), [curl manual](https://curl.se/docs/manual.html).
